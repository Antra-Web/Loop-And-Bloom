Every stitch has a story.

Loop & Bloom started at a kitchen table with one hook and one ball of yarn. Here's how it grew into a studio.

Why we started

Loop & Bloom didn't begin as a business plan. It began as a way to slow down — one evening, one hook, one ball of yarn at a time, after years of days that moved too fast to notice much of anything.

The first pieces were small and a little uneven: a flower with one petal shorter than the rest, a bear with ears that didn't quite match. We kept them anyway, and started giving them away, and people kept asking where they could get one of their own.

What started as a handful of gifts for friends slowly became a studio, then a small team, then a shop with more orders on a good week than we ever expected to hold. The uneven petals are still there if you know where to look — we've just gotten better at making them uneven on purpose.

How we got here

A short timeline of a studio that grew slower than most, on purpose.

Year one

Started with one hook.

A single 4mm hook and a bag of leftover yarn from an unfinished blanket project. The first flower took four attempts before it held its shape.

Year one, later

Turned into a table full of yarn.

What started as one project multiplied into a dozen colors and half-finished pieces spread across every surface of a small kitchen table.

Year two

Then came the first order.

A friend of a friend asked to buy a bouquet as a birthday gift. It felt strange to charge for something that had started as a hobby — but it also felt like the beginning of something real.

Today

And eventually, a little community.

Now Loop & Bloom is a small studio with a handful of makers, a growing shop, and a shelf of custom orders that still gets us a little emotional every time one ships out the door.

What we believe in

Slow, on purpose

We don't rush pieces to hit a quota. A bouquet takes as long as a bouquet takes, even if that means a longer wait for you.

Imperfection is character

A slightly uneven petal or an ear that sits a touch crooked isn't a flaw — it's proof a person, not a machine, made your piece.

Thoughtful, not disposable

We'd rather make something you keep for years than something that ends up in a drawer after one use.
